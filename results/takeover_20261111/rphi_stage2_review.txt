Decision
Proceed with three matched blind 3000-step runs: legacy/guard=0.5,
native/guard=0.5, native/guard=2.0, and separately fit confidence on the fixed
native/guard=0.5 checkpoint. This isolates the encoder at guard=0.5 and the
guard weight within native v2. Comparing archived v1/1000 to v2/3000 alone does
NOT isolate the encoder change. The encoder-by-guard interaction remains
unestimated because there is no legacy/guard=2.0 arm.

What clean/near-clean and guard currently mean
code/rphi_data.py:341-406 samples four equal task slots, then independently draws
control with probabilities 12.5% identity, 12.5% near-clean, 75% actual task.
These are probabilities, not exactly 2+2 examples per batch. At 3000*16 updates,
the expectation is 36000 actual-task crops, 6000 identity, 6000 near-clean;
approximately 9000 actual-task crops per task. Logged control_counts currently
cover the logged batches only, not an exact total exposure census.
Near-clean uses PCG64 Gaussian sigma=1 in uint8 units, rint/clip/uint8; history
padding repeats the same first noisy frame. Identity/near-clean retain nominal
task labels for known models; blind ignores all such metadata (data:96-107,
375-400; model:155-162). For blind, repeated identity controls across nominal
tasks are duplicate evidence, not four independent validation populations.
train_rphi.py:57-69 computes guard = mean_b relu(MAE(pred_b,target_b) -
MAE(current_guide_b,target_b)) across ALL samples. It is not restricted to clean
examples and is neither a local no-harm constraint nor a guarantee on MSE/PSNR.
Increasing guard may discourage beneficial corrections whose L1 does not improve;
it does not establish clean safety. Keep fractions and loss definition fixed in
A/B; changing clean sampling, loss form and guard together would confound results.

Measured weaknesses and checks that distinguish possible causes
Evidence: results/takeover_20261111/rphi_completed/*_eval_metrics.json.
1. v1 blind compression: mean source delta PSNR -0.30205 dB, 5/8 sources have
   worse MSE; worst NPCSVxYpK3k -2.0032 dB. Baseline mean per-frame PSNR is already
   41.373 dB. Mean correction energy 1.6936044e-5 plus cross term -1.6295985e-5
   yields net MSE +6.4006e-7: the correction is not sufficiently aligned with
   the actual error. Known correction energy is 7.4346e-7 (about 23x smaller).
   Diagnose A/B with the EXISTING error_attribution() fields, sourcewise MSE,
   clean controls and clipping fraction; do not just report pooled PSNR.
2. v1 blind near-clean loses about 0.916 dB. Identity MAE is 0.00195445
   (0.4984/255). High-level temporal error-change improves even when near-clean
   fidelity worsens, so temporal smoothing alone must never pass acceptance.
3. v1 blind encoder pools a 96x96 training crop to 64x64 but a 480x832 eval frame
   to 64x64 before the same feature extractor (model:96-104). Native v2 removes
   this pixel-scale mismatch (condition_v2:44-68); its final globally pooled code
   can STILL change with content/extent. Measure identical crop vs full-frame
   code/output on held dev examples; do not assert complete scale invariance.
4. Deblur is weak in known too (+0.08550 dB; blind +0.10158 dB), so blind condition
   extraction is not the sole demonstrated cause. Blind deblur correction energy
   is only 2.3523e-6 against baseline MSE 1.0104e-3. Possible under-training,
   conservative optimization and spatially unaligned K3 context remain hypotheses.
   The model stacks three frames through convolutions, without optical-flow or
   deformable alignment. Inspect per-source motion/edge examples and fixed crop
   validation curves; do not change architecture during this guard experiment.
5. Materialized v1 has only one constant isotropic Gaussian blur severity and one
   H264 CRF per source/window; no real motion blur, ordered mixtures, severity
   changes or new frozen test. CRF is NOT sufficient to explain damage: CRF=21
   sources include both gains and losses. Stratification is descriptive with n=8.

Evaluation and timing contract
Train manifest: 128 task rows, 32 unique source IDs and 32 source hashes.
Dev manifest: 32 task rows, 8 unique sources. Each has one 22-frame, 24 fps,
480x832 original-recording RGB proxy. Independent sample size is 8 sources,
not 32 task rows, hundreds of frames or millions of pixels. Dev material spans
about 7.33 seconds total (8*22/24); enough for a diagnostic pilot, insufficient
for claims of omni generalization, scene cuts, long video, drift or robustness.
Keep source separation and materialization receipt/file/pixel hashes enforced.
The existing evaluator correctly preserves consecutive causal history even when
scoring strided frames (eval:296-333), uses common GT-derived flow/masks, and
aggregates windows within source then equal source weights (eval:401-410).
Current stride=4 only scores [0,4,8,12,16,20], hence five FOUR-frame-gap temporal
pairs. The authorized first screening uses stride=4 for all three arms and the
frozen head on ALL 8 sources/32 rows, identity and near-clean, same LPIPS/flow.
This is not adjacent-frame flicker acceptance. A selected candidate and its
relevant comparison must later use stride=1, all 22 frames, for that claim.
Report flow valid coverage and non-null valid pairs; no silent missing LPIPS or
flow fallback may be compared as if the same metric. A stride-4 early diagnostic
may be retained but is not adjacent-frame flicker validation.
Verify C's restored RGB agrees with its source checkpoint under the same batch,
input, backend and dtype, and fidelity state SHA is exactly unchanged. Its NLL,
coverage, variance/error bins and controls must use RAW unclamped errors; quality
still uses the common clipped RGB metric contract. Compare calibration to a
training-fitted constant-variance baseline, not just to the old joint-trained
head. Do not use confidence for restoration gating or softDC in this round.

Observed v1 times: blind train/validation/save 415.9 s per 1000 steps; full dev
stride-4 eval 657.1 s (known 607.9 s, confidence 790.7 s). A rough planning scale
is ~21 min per 3000-step train and ~40 min per stride-1 plain eval, before native
encoder overhead; benchmark one short native/full-frame case before promising
an ETA. Two full arms plus calibration/evaluation can exceed the old 5400-second
completion waiter. Set a finite stage-specific budget from the measured smoke
and expected rows; timeout is an incomplete run, never success.

Stop / continue / promotion rules
Immediate technical stop for the affected queue only: nonfinite losses/gradients
or output, data/hash/split mismatch, reload mismatch, missing required tasks,
incompatible encoder checkpoint reconstruction, invalid evaluation support, or
any changed fidelity tensor during calibration. Preserve failure.json/logs and
last complete checkpoint; no auto-retry over existing paths and no killing
unrelated processes. Unless there is a predeclared catastrophic safety condition,
finish the fixed A/B budget rather than stopping one arm on noisier proxy quality.
At completion, publish sourcewise model-vs-input and B-vs-A deltas, restoration
AND controls AND temporal results. Candidate selection must prioritize fixing
compression/clean harm without sacrificing the useful denoise gain; a pooled
four-task mean dominated by denoise is not acceptance. Proposed conservative H3
promotion gate: no task has worse source-mean MSE than its input baseline;
near-clean source-mean MSE does not increase; worst-source losses and absolute
identity MAE/MSE meet an explicitly predeclared application tolerance; no temporal
or perceptual regression is hidden by a fidelity average. Set those tolerances
BEFORE seeing stage-2 results. If neither arm passes, keep both as diagnostic
checkpoints and investigate per-task gradients/data/temporal alignment next;
do not automatically train longer, add more losses, or couple confidence/H3.
With only 8 dev sources, paired source bootstrap/win counts are descriptive;
promising gains still require a fresh frozen-source set and another seed before
claiming structural superiority. These are development promotion rules, not an
assertion that current evidence meets them.
